Project 03

Timer Box

A timer-controlled dispenser that uses a motorized rubber wheel and a custom spring mechanism to release paper flyers at a scheduled time.

Device

Using a toy money gun as inspiration, the Timer Box was designed to dispense paper flyers automatically at a set time. A timer and controller activate a motor-driven rubber wheel, which grips the top flyer and pushes it out of the enclosure.

Actuation

Timed motor control

A timer and controller trigger the dispensing motor at the scheduled time.

Feed mechanism

Spring-loaded flyers

A custom spring mechanism keeps the stack pressed against the rubber drive wheel.

Packaging

Integrated component cover

A fitted cover protects the battery pack, timer, controller, motor, and their wiring during operation.

Dedicated mounts were designed for every component to keep the assembly rugged and sturdy. The components are fixed to the base with plastic pins and CA glue, while the cover further protects the components and their wiring during operation.

Iteration

The mechanism went through multiple prototypes. The first version was too flimsy to maintain consistent pressure on the flyer stack, causing the paper to shift and jam before reaching the rubber wheel.

The redesigned version uses thicker walls and added chamfers for greater stiffness and smoother paper travel. The spring mechanism was also revised to apply more reliable pressure, while the new component cover stabilized the battery pack, timer, controller, and motor.

The internal components required extensive iteration as well, particularly the motor mount and component cover. Their snap-fit clips had to be strong enough to hold each part securely without breaking during assembly or operation. Repeated CAD revisions were used to refine the clip geometry, wall thickness, and fit until the mounts were both durable and easy to assemble. Together, these changes produced a stronger and more dependable dispensing system.

Skills & tools

  • CAD and SolidWorks: designed the enclosure, spring mechanism, motor mount, component cover, and snap-fit clips through multiple iterations.
  • Soldering: assembled the electrical connections between the timer, controller, motor, and battery pack.
  • Design for assembly: developed integrated mounts that kept each component stable while allowing the box to be assembled efficiently.
